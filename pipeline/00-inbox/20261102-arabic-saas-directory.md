# Idea Brief: Arabic-ready software directory with paid featured listings

- **Id:** 20261102-arabic-saas-directory

- **Method playbook:** research/playbooks/paid-niche-directory.md
- **Date:** 2026-11-02

## What it is
A bilingual directory of business software that genuinely supports
Arabic (RTL interface, Arabic invoices, Hijri dates, GCC VAT, local
payment gateways), organised by job (accounting, HR and payroll, POS,
e-commerce, CRM), with a verified "Arabic-ready" checklist per tool.
Free listings for every tool; revenue from featured placement at USD 79 a
month or USD 790 a year per category, sponsored comparison pages, and
affiliate links where vendors run programmes. Hosted on a sub-domain of
the studio domain. Vendors are reached by studio-domain email.

## Who pays, how much, how often
Marketing managers at SaaS vendors selling into the GCC (regional players
such as accounting, HR and e-commerce platforms, and global vendors with
Arabic localisation). Price anchors: OpenAlternative charges USD 197 a
month per featured listing (35% of USD 6,500 MRR); a UK directory took
GBP 1,200 a month from premium listings and GBP 400 from featured
placements before adding pay-per-lead; AI Directories shows about
USD 8,270 trailing-30-day revenue from one-time submission packages;
IndieNeed crossed USD 250 with paid skip-the-queue listings. Monthly or
annual, renewed while the directory sends traffic.

## Why it works now
Four independent first-person reports show small directories earning
USD 250 to 6,500 a month from featured listings and sponsorships, and the
model is agent-friendly: build the site, verify each tool against a
public checklist, email vendors (business addresses) to claim and
upgrade their listing, publish comparison pages. "Does this software work
in Arabic" is a real buying question in the GCC with no verified answer
site; vendors have budget for GCC visibility and are easier to reach than
consumers. The free listing is the outreach hook; the vendor's own
"claim your listing" link gives a measurable signal in the validation.

## What would kill it
- Traffic takes months: OpenAlternative needed about a year; featured listings only sell once the directory sends visitors, so the first months earn little.
- Google organic cannot be the only channel (rule 9): vendor email and vendor-shared listings must carry the early traffic.
- Directory saturation and AI answer engines reduce clicks to list pages.
- Verification burden: claims about Arabic support must be tested, not copied, or the directory loses trust.

## Platform and payout check (from research/platform-rules.md and management/payments.md)

## Distribution
Channel 1: studio-domain email to 20 then 30 marketing managers at SaaS
vendors with Arabic localisation, offering the free verified listing and
the featured upgrade; the validation counts "claim listing" replies and
paid upgrades. First ten customers: those vendors. Second channel: the
vendors' own sharing of their listing badge, plus bilingual comparison
pages ("best Arabic accounting software for Qatar") built for organic
search and for the studio's other services' prospects (Salla reporting,
automation, Excel clients receive the directory link in deliveries).
