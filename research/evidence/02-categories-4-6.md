## Category 4: Services and gigs

### Platform data (primary, buyer side)
- Fiverr Q2 2026 (reported 2026-07-29): annual active buyers 2.7M vs 3.4M a year earlier (-21.9%); annual spend per buyer $368 (+15.6%); marketplace take rate 28.0%; revenue $97.8M (-10.0% vs $108.6M); marketplace revenue $63.1M (-15.5%); services revenue $34.6M (+2.0%). One recap says -20.6% buyers (rounding/definition difference; Fiverr's own figure is -21.9%).
- Fiverr trend: Dec 2024 3.6M buyers -> Dec 2025 3.1M (-13.6%) -> Mar 2026 2.9M -> Jun 2026 2.7M. Spend per buyer $302 (FY24) -> $342 (FY25) -> $356 (Q1 26) -> $368 (Q2 26). FY2025 revenue $430.9M (+10.1%); take rate 27.7% (FY25). H1 2026 revenue ($105.5M + $97.8M = $203.3M) is -5.8% vs H1 2025 ($107.2M + $108.6M = $215.8M) (derived).
- Fiverr management says AI absorbs high-volume, low-value, transactional tasks and shifts demand to bigger projects; clients with $1,000+ projects +13% TTM. Implication for a new small operator: the entry-level gig segment (the one a newcomer can reach) is the shrinking one.
- Implied marketplace spend: 2.7M x $368 = ~$0.99B per year (derived). Fiverr's active SELLER count and seller earnings distribution were not found (UNVERIFIED). Average buyer spend of $368/yr means gig volume per buyer is small.
- One analysis claims FY2026 revenue guidance of -14% to -17%; this does not square with H1 2026 at -5.8% and I could not confirm it (UNVERIFIED).
- Upwork Q2 2026 (reported 2026-08-10): GSV $966.4M (-4% YoY); active clients 763K (-4%); GSV per active client $5,230 (+5%, record); revenue $191.7M (-2%); GAAP net income $25.4M; adjusted EBITDA $64.1M; AI-related GSV ~$330M (+22%); Business Plus GSV ~$64M (+174%). Guidance FY2026 revenue $730-750M vs FY2025 $787.8M, i.e. -4.8% to -7.3% (derived). One headline (StaffingIndustry) reads "revenue falls 17"; this conflicts with -2% and is probably a typo or a different metric (UNVERIFIED).
- Upwork take rate: MarketBeat says 19.8%; Panabee says 19.6% (+110bp). Derived 191.7/966.4 = 19.84%. Treat ~19.6-19.8% as company-level (client fees + freelancer fees combined), not what a freelancer pays.
- Upwork FY2025: active clients 785K at Dec 31 2025 (812K in Q1 2025; 794K in Q3 2025); Q4 GSV per active client $5,129 (+7%); revenue $787.8M (+2%); FCF $223M.
- Upwork fee mechanics (third-party guides, not Upwork's own page): freelancer service fee variable 0-15% per contract from May 1 2025 (previously flat 10%); Connects $0.15 each; Freelancer Plus gets 100 Connects/month, Basic 10 free. A different search summary said a variable fee is rolling to more categories during 2026, so the rollout date is inconsistent across sources (M-L). Company says it expects take rate to keep rising via dynamic pricing, Connects, Ads, Business Plus.
- Fiverr seller commission: flat 20% per third-party sources (Fiverr fee page not retrieved).

### Income surveys
- Upwork Research Institute Future Workforce Index (US skilled knowledge workers, survey-based): 2025 edition said full-time-only freelancers had median $85K vs $80K for employees; 2026 edition (n=2,400, Mar-Apr 2026) says employees $109K vs freelancers $101K, bottom-quartile freelancers $80K. These are established US knowledge workers, a survivor-biased population; they say nothing about new small operators or about someone based in Qatar. Do not use as an expected-income figure.
- Payoneer: no 2025/2026 freelancer income report found. Secondary hits only: ~$22-24/hr (undated, via Clockify), $21/hr in 2020 (via Ureed), UAE median $78,150 attributed to Payoneer 2024 (via Jobbers.io, low trust).

### Time to first dollar / month-6 range
- Fiverr: earnifyhub review (blog, 2026) says 4-10 weeks for first organic order and a $0-200/mo band for new sellers with no reviews, $200-1,000 at Level 1; a $1,200 "median" for Fiverr-primary sellers is quoted with no named source. Treat all as L.
- Upwork: no time-to-first-contract data found (UNVERIFIED). Local B2B / done-for-you services: no data found (UNVERIFIED).

### Share earning meaningfully

## Category 5: Software

### Revenue distribution
- BigIdeasDB "State of Indie SaaS Revenue 2026" (TrustMRR-style self-reported data): 3,787 revenue-generating bootstrapped SaaS; median MRR $145; average $4,298; ~70% under $1,000 MRR; 6.1% at or above $10K MRR; profitable products median ~$4,200 MRR. Another benchmark blog quotes a 2025 cohort with median $168 MRR and 23.5% over $1K, noting survivorship. These exclude non-revenue products, so they overstate the typical outcome.
- Separate analysis of 10,150 TrustMRR products (exact page not identified in my results; the search surfaced mean.ceo and techstartups.com pages for the same topic): 26.1% never recorded a payment; only 49.1% had revenue in the prior 30 days; median among earners $169. TrustMRR is self-selected (founders who choose to display revenue).
- Indie Hackers analysis of 5,079 Stripe-verified startups (seen only via a French Substack that cites it): median monthly revenue $148 in year 1 and $2,399 in year 4. Original IH post URL appeared in results but I could not read it, and its date is unknown.
- quasa.io write-up of TrustMRR data (low trust, conflicts with $145 median): median $334/mo, 20% at $0, avg 5 months from $0 to first $1 MRR, ~6 more months from $1K to $10K MRR.
- Triangulation: median revenue-generating indie SaaS is ~$150/mo in year 1, so a month-6 operator should plan on ~$0-$300 MRR with a minority (~25-30% of those who earn anything) above $1K.
- Time to first revenue: 5 months avg (quasa, L), 6-12 months (DEV Community table, unsourced, L), 12-18 months to "meaningful" revenue (mean.ceo citing RockingWeb, L). None is a survey-grade median (UNVERIFIED).

### Acquire.com / marketplace data
- BigIdeasDB "State of SaaS Acquisitions 2026" (615 SaaS listings, asking prices not closed sales): median ask $190K; IQR $75K-$500K; avg ~$431K; median TTM revenue $118K; median TTM profit $55K. Beancount.io (July 2026) says 600+ listing average ~$484K (conflict with $431K; methodology weak) and sub-$1M ARR sells at ~2.5x-4x revenue. Listings are survivors who reached a saleable size; they are not the median new micro-SaaS.
- Flippa data: not retrieved (UNVERIFIED).

### Chrome Web Store
- Distribution: ~87% of extensions under 1,000 users and half under 16 users (2019 Extension Monitor via 9to5Google); a Feb 2024 snapshot says 69.5% under 100 users and 7.7% over 1,000; DebugBear also ~70% under 100 users. Over 90% of extensions are free (ITWeb, 2019 study). No current Chrome Web Store monetisation/paid-share data found (UNVERIFIED). (Background knowledge, not verified this session: Google retired Chrome Web Store payments, so paid extensions need external billing.)

### Discord/Telegram bots, paid APIs, data products
- No evidence retrieved (searches not run). UNVERIFIED.

### 2025-26 changes
- AI coding tools make building cheaper; whether that has pushed median MRR down or crowding up: no primary data found (UNVERIFIED). Two post titles surfaced ("Are indie hackers really going extinct?", "SaaS Is the Only Way Out") but I did not read them and do not cite them.

### Course-seller flag
- Mixed. TrustMRR/Stripe-verified datasets are payment-connected (better than screenshots) but self-selected; surrounding "micro-SaaS examples that print money" content is guru/SEO. MicroConf/Indie Hackers "State of Independent SaaS" survey itself was NOT found; the survey named in the brief does not appear to exist in this form (UNVERIFIED).

### Qatar
- Polar (merchant of record built on Stripe Connect Express) lists Qatar among supported payout countries (docs page seen via search excerpt). Stripe changelog dated 2026-01-28 added Qatar bank accounts as a cross-border payout destination (public preview); this concerns payout destinations, not necessarily creating a standard Stripe account as a Qatar business. Bandcamp, Algora, ElevenLabs Stripe-Connect lists also include Qatar (secondary). Paddle and Lemon Squeezy Qatar support: UNVERIFIED. Practical read: a Qatar-based builder can plausibly sell via a merchant of record (Polar) and receive payouts to a Qatar bank, but confirm onboarding rules in Polar's docs first.

## Category 6: Arbitrage and resale

### Amazon FBA
- Jungle Scout State of the Amazon Seller 2025 (nearly 1,500 respondents, surveyed Jan 10-27 2025; solo/SMB and enterprise): majority of sellers have margins above 10%, one-third above 20%; 38% cite higher shipping costs as a top challenge; China sourcing -30%, US sourcing up sharply (+97% per one summary). A 2026 edition was NOT found. Jungle Scout 2024: 58% profitable within first year. Jungle Scout sells seller tools and recruits respondents from its audience, so the sample is skewed to active, engaged sellers (survivorship).
- Secondary figures attributed to Jungle Scout (year unclear): average SMB monthly sales $11,671, 21% net margin, ~$2,451 monthly profit. These describe established SMBs, not month-6 operators.
- New-seller ranges (vendor blogs, L): RedStag says most new sellers make $500-2,000 monthly revenue with net profit typically under $500 in the first six months; Sumtracker says 52% of sellers exceed $1,000/mo revenue and 31% earn under $500/mo; amzprep says most sellers make $1,000-25,000 monthly revenue and $200-5,000 profit. Revenue and profit are conflated in places.
- Time to first dollar: no primary data (UNVERIFIED).
- 2026 fee changes (third-party summaries, no Amazon page retrieved): base FBA fulfilment fees up Jan 15 2026 (avg +$0.08/unit; small standard ~+$0.51, large standard ~+$0.31, extra-large ~-$2.08); fee brackets now split by price (<$10, $10-50, >$50); 3.5% fuel and logistics surcharge on FBA fulfilment fees (US and Canada) from Apr 17 2026 (MCF and Buy with Prime from May 2 2026); low-price FBA discount for <$10 items $0.77 -> $0.86; low-inventory-level fee at FNSKU level; aged-inventory surcharges from 181 days.

### eBay
- eBay Q2 2026 (quarter ended Jun 30 2026; via secondary coverage of release/call): GMV $22.4B (+15%); revenue $3.1B (+15%); 136M active buyers (+2% YoY, flat QoQ), US buyers +6%; take rate ~14%; advertising revenue $596M (2.7% of GMV); Depop acquisition. No data on seller-income distribution or new-seller outcomes (UNVERIFIED).

### Dropshipping
- No credible failure-rate study found. "80-90% of stores fail" has no traceable source; "18% success" is attributed to Dropship.io (a tool vendor) surveying 1,253 FAILED store owners; Shopify's 2020 survey of 932 dropshippers is the closest real data and sorted by revenue (low performers under $25K/yr), not profit.
- De minimis: China/Hong Kong lost commercial de minimis May 2 2025; suspension extended to all countries Aug 29 2025 (one guide); the same guide claims CBP made it indefinite in June 2026 (single source, UNVERIFIED). Estimates of 10-30% higher dropshipper costs (estimate, not measured); supplier transit time 8-10 days -> 24-28 days (one anecdote); one seller's US share fell from ~60% to ~20-30% (anecdote). In 2024 more than 90% of US inbound packages used de minimis (~4M/day) per one source.

### Domain flipping
- No NameBio primary data retrieved. Secondary: average sale price ~$16,233 attributed to DNJournal (H1 2025 per one source, FY2025 per another; skewed by premium sales); median sale quoted as $500-800 by one source and ~$2,500 by another (conflict, no NameBio tie). 2024 market "$185M across 144,700 domains" (avg $1,281) with unconfirmed origin. New-gTLD monthly sales (TLD Investors/NameBio): about $224K in Jan 2025.
- Sell-through rate: personal sellers quote ~1-3%/yr; a broker's marketplace averages ~6.6%; Namecheap treats 1% as typical for large portfolios. A 2026 guide says no authoritative benchmark exists for median profit. Implication: expected time to first sale for a small portfolio is long (months to years); at 1-3% STR, a 100-domain portfolio yields ~1-3 sales/yr (derived).

### Ticket resale, cross-border resale
- No evidence retrieved. UNVERIFIED.

### Course-seller flag
- Mostly vendors and gurus: Jungle Scout, amzprep, RedStag, Sumtracker are tool/3PL vendors; dropshipping stats come from tool vendors and "success rate" blogs; domain stats from forums and registrar/brokerage blogs. Flag: high.

### Qatar
- Amazon: registration country and payout country are separate lists (Amazon staff reply in seller forum; Iceland example). Sole traders/business sellers need proof of business establishment in a listed country, a local phone number and an internationally chargeable credit card. Whether Qatar is on the registration or disbursement lists: UNVERIFIED (Viral Launch list updated Sep 8 2025 and Amazon help page G201468460 exist but I could not read them). Workaround (company in an accepted country) is third-party advice, not Amazon policy. Amazon.ae/.sa selling from Qatar: UNVERIFIED. eBay/Etsy/shipping from Qatar: UNVERIFIED.
